## Which English tests do Irish universities accept?

Most of the Irish universities we checked accept IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT, the Duolingo English Test and PTE Academic. Several also take Cambridge exams, LanguageCert, ETAPP or Trinity ISE. The accepted list and the scores differ by university and sometimes by course, so read your course page before you book anything.

| University | IELTS Academic | Other tests and notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| University of Galway | 6.5 overall, no section below 6.0 | Lists a two-year validity for its accepted tests. |
| UCC, postgraduate | 6.5 overall, 6.0 in each skill. Online IELTS and the one-skill retake are accepted. | Duolingo 120 with 110 in each subscore. A small number of programmes ask for more. |
| Maynooth, 2027/28 intake (non-EU undergraduate and postgraduate) | 6.5, no band below 6.0 | Duolingo 120 with 110 in each subscore. PTE Academic 63 with 59 in each skill. TOEFL iBT 90 with at least 21 in writing and 19 elsewhere. TOEFL Home Edition not accepted. |
| DCU, undergraduate | 6.5, no component below 6.0 | TOEFL iBT 92. PTE Academic 63 with 59 in each component. Duolingo 120 with 110 in each subscore. TOEFL Home Edition not accepted. Results must come from one sitting. |
| TU Dublin, undergraduate | 6.0, no band below 6 | TOEFL iBT 80 for tests taken before 21 January 2026, and a new scoring scheme after that date. Duolingo 120 with 110 in each subscore. PTE Academic 65. Postgraduate requirements are on each programme page. |

These are the minimums each university published on 23 November 2026. Individual programmes can set a higher score, and several pages say so.

## What English score does the visa require?

The Irish Immigration Service sets its own minimums for visa purposes. For most courses they are IELTS Academic 5.0, TOEFL iBT 61, PTE Academic 30 and Duolingo 75. Your college's standard may be higher, and the visa office does not replace it. The certificate must be issued within two years of your expected course start.

| Test | Visa minimum for most courses |
| --- | --- |
| IELTS Academic, including UKVI IELTS | 5.0 |
| TOEFL iBT, including the At Home Edition | 61 |
| PTE Academic | 30 |
| Duolingo English Test | 75 |
| Oxford Test of English | 100 |
| LanguageCert Academic | 60 |
| Cambridge English | A pass in First Certificate in English, or a higher Cambridge exam |
| ETAPP, IELCA, Skills for English, LanguageCert International ESOL | B2 |
| Trinity College London ISE | ISE II (B2) |

The Immigration Service says its table is for visa purposes only, and that each student must also meet the requirements of their college. Lower minimums apply to second-level, foundation and preparatory English courses. The Immigration Service says it also accepts results from online tests taken with the test providers.

A test the visa office accepts may not be accepted by your university. Maynooth and DCU both say they do not accept the TOEFL Home Edition, though the visa table lists it. Check both lists for the test you plan to take.

## Which test should I take?

Choose a test that every university on your shortlist accepts, and aim for the highest score any of them asks for. IELTS Academic appears on all five university lists above and on the visa list. Check how each one treats online versions and retakes before you book, because the rules differ.

Three differences worth checking:

- **One sitting or several.** DCU and TU Dublin say your scores must come from a single sitting.
- **Retakes.** UCC accepts the IELTS one-skill retake for postgraduate entry. Maynooth does not accept it for non-EU undergraduate applicants.
- **At-home tests.** Maynooth and DCU don't take the TOEFL Home Edition. The visa table does.

If your courses are at different universities, the strictest list sets the test and the score you need.

## When should I take the test?

Take it early enough that the result is under two years old when your course starts, and ready before you apply for the visa. The two years run back from your expected start date, at the universities and at the visa office. TU Dublin suggests preparing for the test 9 to 12 months before you apply.

You may not need the result on the day you apply. TU Dublin says you can submit an application without English test results and add them later. Other universities may differ, so read the instructions in the application portal.

The test date is only part of the timeline. Our guide to [Irish university intakes and deadlines for 2027](/guides/irish-university-intakes-and-deadlines-2027) shows the dates around it.

## Do I need an English test at all?

Sometimes not. UCC normally treats applicants from, or holding a level 8 or 9 qualification taught in, a listed English-speaking country as meeting its requirement. Its list includes Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya. Maynooth names Nigeria and Ghana for a bachelor's degree taught in English. Other universities decide case by case.

University of Galway lets some applicants use secondary-level exam results in place of a test. Its list includes WASSCE/WAEC for Ghana and the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education for Kenya, with grade requirements on its page. DCU says it may accept evidence of an education taught in English in exceptional circumstances. TU Dublin may accept a degree taken through English for postgraduate applicants.

A university exemption doesn't settle the visa question. Maynooth says applicants may still need a recognised English certificate for visa purposes. The Immigration Service's English page lists accepted tests and says nothing about exemptions by country. Ask the visa office what evidence it will accept in your situation, and ask the university in writing whether it waives the test for your qualification.

## What if my English score is not high enough yet?

Some students take a preparatory English course before the main course. For visa purposes that course can last no more than six months. You submit a detailed study plan with dates for both courses, and you pay the fees for the preparatory course and the first year of the main course in full, with proof of payment.

Universities offer their own routes too. UCD's International Foundation Year is a one-year programme for international students who don't yet meet direct-entry requirements, and it combines study skills, English language training and academic study.

Before you choose, check whether the preparatory course and the main course both meet the Immigration Service's rules. Our guide to [studying in Ireland from Nigeria](/guides/studying-in-ireland-from-nigeria) covers how fees and the letter of acceptance fit into the visa application.
