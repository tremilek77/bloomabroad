## Why can a visa be refused when I already have an offer?

A university offer and a visa are two separate decisions. The college decides whether to admit you. Ireland's immigration authorities decide whether to grant the visa, and they judge it on the documents in your visa application. The Irish Immigration Service says that sending your documents does not guarantee success.

Your letter of acceptance is one paper in that file. The proof of fee payment, your academic and English evidence and the rest sit beside it. If a required document is missing, the Immigration Service says your application may be refused.

Two practical points from the same pages. The visa fee isn't refunded if your application is refused. And the Immigration Service advises you not to pay for a travel ticket before you have a decision.

## What reasons for refusal does Ireland give?

The Irish authorities name five problems. They are incomplete documents, a missing certified translation, information they cannot verify, an earlier visa refusal you did not disclose, and false or misleading information. Only your own refusal letter tells you why your application was refused.

| What goes wrong | What the official pages say |
| --- | --- |
| Missing documents | If you don't send the required documents, your application may be refused. |
| No certified translation | A document not in English or Irish needs a full certified translation. The Abuja embassy lists a missing translation as a ground for refusal. |
| Information that can't be checked | The Abuja embassy can refuse when it cannot verify what you supplied. Letters from a college, employer or other organisation must be original, on headed paper, with contact details that can be checked. |
| An earlier refusal you didn't mention | Any earlier visa refusal, from any country, must be disclosed with the original refusal letter. Leaving it out leads to refusal. |
| False or misleading information | Your application may be refused, and in some cases you cannot appeal and may be blocked from getting an Irish visa for five years. |

Two more points are not described as grounds for refusal, but the Immigration Service asks you to explain them in writing. One is any gap in your education history. The other is a course that doesn't follow on from your earlier study or work. For that one, your application letter must give valid reasons and support them with evidence if you have it.

The pages we checked don't say how often each problem leads to a refusal, so the order above carries no meaning.

## How serious is false or missing information?

It is the most serious one on the list. The Irish Immigration Service says false or misleading information may mean a refusal with no right of appeal and a block of up to five years on getting an Irish visa. The Embassy of Ireland in Abuja says it applies a zero tolerance policy to fraudulent applications.

The embassy treats withheld information as deception too. It gives an earlier visa refusal as its example. It adds that when an application is sent as part of a group, every application in that group may be refused without appeal.

If you have had a refusal for any country, say so and include the original letter. The Immigration Service lists "Visa Refusals" as its own document heading on the study visa page, so there is a place for it.

## What do the refusal figures show?

The Irish Examiner reported Department of Justice figures on 17 October 2026. This year 506 of 1,393 Nigerian long-stay study visa applications were refused, a rate of 36%. In 2025 the figure was 445 of 1,106, or 40%. The numbers count refusals and give no reasons.

A refusal rate describes people who already applied. It cannot tell you how an officer will read your documents, and it says nothing about the reasons behind any single refusal. The same article gave no reasons specific to Nigeria.

A Department of Justice spokesperson told the paper that applicants should provide all relevant information in support of their application. The spokesperson added that people who follow the published guidelines have an improved prospect of a positive decision. That matches the advice on the Immigration Service's own pages: prepare carefully, because you carry the responsibility for the information you send.

## What should I do if my visa is refused?

Read the refusal letter first. It gives the reasons and says whether you can appeal. An appeal costs nothing but must reach the Immigration Service within two months of the date on the letter. You can appeal once for each application. A new application is also open to you.

1. **Read the letter closely.** It states the reasons and whether an appeal is allowed. If false or misleading information was the reason, it will say whether you can appeal and whether a five-year block applies.
2. **Choose between an appeal and a new application.** If your appeal arrives late, the original decision cannot be changed, though you can still submit and pay for a new application.
3. **Write the appeal letter.** Include your full name, postal address, personal email address and Visa Application Transaction Number. Explain in detail why you think the decision should change, refer to the reasons in the refusal letter, and add any new information that matters. Sign and date it.
4. **Add your documents.** Originals, with certified translations for anything not in English or Irish. Don't include your passport unless the refusal letter tells you to.
5. **Send it** to the Visa Appeals Officer at the address in the refusal letter.

If the student is under 18, a parent or legal guardian has to make the appeal. A student under 18 cannot appeal alone.

Appeals take time. On 17 November 2026 the Dublin visa office's page showed it was processing study appeals received on 6 October 2025, and processing times change through the year. If an appeal fails, you'll get a second refusal letter and you cannot appeal again. You can still submit and pay for a new application.

## How can I check my application before I send it?

Read the official page for your visa type and tick off every document it lists. Each one should be legible and in English or Irish, with a certified translation if it isn't. Disclose any earlier refusal with the original letter. Explain any gap in your studies, or a change of course, in your application letter.

A few details from the Immigration Service's page catch people out:

- Letters from a business, college or other organisation must be original and on headed paper with a fixed-line phone number, a website, a named contact person and a written signature. Electronic signatures are not accepted.
- Email addresses ending in Yahoo or Hotmail are not accepted on those letters.
- Documents must be on paper. USB sticks, memory cards and file-sharing links can't be opened.
- Your Application Summary Form tells you where to send your application and how to pay.
