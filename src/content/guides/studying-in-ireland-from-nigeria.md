## Do I need a visa to study in Ireland from Nigeria?

Yes. Nigeria is on Ireland's list of visa-required nationalities, so Nigerian citizens need a visa before they travel. For a course longer than three months, that means a long-stay study visa. You apply online through AVATS, the Automated Visa Application and Tracking System, from Nigeria or the country where you legally live.

Apply up to three months before you plan to travel. If you will visit another country on the way to Ireland, you need that country's visa in your passport before you apply for the Irish one.

The official list of visa-required nationalities can change. The version we checked is dated May 2025, so read the current one linked under Sources.

## What are the steps to study in Ireland from Nigeria?

There are seven steps, from choosing an eligible course to registering with immigration after you arrive. They run in a fixed order, and your fees must be paid to the required level before you apply. Registration applies if you will stay longer than 90 days.

1. **Choose a course that meets Ireland's rules.** The Irish Immigration Service says to read its policy on non-EEA nationals studying in Ireland and to make sure you meet it before you apply for a visa.
2. **Apply to the college.** Check the entry requirements for your course first. Admission decisions are made by the college.
3. **Get your letter of acceptance.** It must confirm full-time study with at least 15 hours of organised daytime tuition a week, and state the fees payable and the fees paid.
4. **Pay your fees to the required level.** If the fees are under €6,000, pay them in full before you apply. If they are over €6,000, pay at least €6,000. Your college may ask for more.
5. **Apply for your visa.** Complete the application on AVATS, pay the visa fee, and send your signed summary form with your documents. The summary form tells you where to send them.
6. **Wait for the decision.** Do not buy travel tickets until you know the outcome.
7. **Travel and register.** Students staying longer than 90 days must register with immigration after they arrive.

## What does a study visa application include?

Ireland asks for a signed AVATS summary form, the visa fee, your passport and a set of supporting documents. Those include a letter of acceptance and proof of fee payment. Evidence of your academic and English ability, medical insurance and an application letter are on the list too. Missing documents can lead to refusal.

The official page lists every document. The main ones are:

- **Passport:** valid for at least 12 months after the date you plan to arrive, plus a copy of every page of any previous passports.
- **Two passport photographs:** no more than six months old.
- **Letter of acceptance and proof of fee payment** from your college.
- **Evidence of academic ability:** for example, previous exam results or qualifications.
- **Evidence of English ability:** a certificate from the test provider showing your results.
- **Private medical insurance:** your college may arrange it and say so in your letter of acceptance.
- **Application letter:** your reasons for coming, your dates, any family in Ireland or another EU country, and your commitments about the conditions of your visa.
- **An account of any gaps** since your last period of full-time education, with your employment history.
- **Details of any previous visa refusal** for any country, with the original refusal letter.
- **Finances:** the official page explains what to provide, including the summary of finances form.

Send original documents on paper. The Irish Immigration Service does not accept documents on USB sticks, memory cards or file-sharing sites, and anything not in English or Irish needs a full certified translation.

## How much money does Ireland ask students to show?

Visa-required students on academic courses starting after 1 July 2023 must show immediate access to at least €10,000 for a one-year course. Ireland gives that as the estimated cost of living for a year. Longer courses need ready access to the same amount for each later year. Courses under eight months use €833 a month, or €6,665 if that is lower.

These figures are for living costs and are in addition to your course fees. The Irish Immigration Service lists the evidence it accepts on its student finances page, linked under Sources, and updates the figures from time to time.

BloomAbroad doesn't prepare, source or advise on financial documents, and we will never ask you for money to "show funds". Be cautious of anyone who offers to.

## How long does a study visa decision take?

The Irish Immigration Service says applicants can expect a decision within approximately eight weeks of the application reaching the visa office, embassy or consulate. It can take longer if documents are missing or need to be checked. Applications are processed in the order they arrive, so do not buy tickets before you have the outcome.

Processing times vary between countries and application types, and can change at busy times of year. Your application office can tell you where your application stands.

## What do I do when I arrive in Ireland?

Students staying in Ireland for longer than 90 days must register with the immigration authorities after they arrive. Visa-required students are not asked to show proof of finances again at registration, because the visa office checks this during the visa application. Check the official registration steps before you travel.

Keep your passport, your letter of acceptance and your other original documents with you when you travel.

## What if the student is under 18?

Unaccompanied students under 18 need extra documents. These include the birth certificate, notarised consent from both parents or legal guardians, and accommodation details. The Irish Immigration Service also says under-18s need a guardian in Ireland. Where a parent is not staying with the student, the school seeks Garda clearance for the place where the student will live.

The official page sets out every requirement for under-18s, including what applies when only one parent has custody or when a parent travels with the student.

## What mistakes delay or sink an application?

Missing documents, an undisclosed visa refusal and false or misleading information are the problems the Irish Immigration Service names. Missing documents may lead to refusal. Not disclosing a previous refusal will lead to refusal. False information can lead to refusal and a five-year block on getting an Irish visa.

Two more points need a clear, honest explanation:

- **Gaps in your education history.** Account for the time, with your employment history.
- **A course that doesn't follow on from your past study or work.** Your application letter must give your reasons and support them with evidence if you have it.
