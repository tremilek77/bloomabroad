## Do students from Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya need a visa to study in Ireland?

Yes. Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya are all on Ireland's list of visa-required nationalities. For a course longer than three months you need a long-stay study visa, and you apply for it online through AVATS, the Automated Visa Application and Tracking System. The list can change, and the version we checked is dated May 2025.

Apply up to three months before you plan to travel. The visa fee is not refunded if your application is withdrawn or refused, and the Irish Immigration Service advises you not to buy a travel ticket before you have a decision.

For the full order of steps, read our guide to [studying in Ireland from Nigeria](/guides/studying-in-ireland-from-nigeria). The Immigration Service's process starts with the same online system, AVATS, whichever country you apply from.

## Where do I hand in my documents in Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya?

It depends on your country. The Embassy of Ireland in Abuja says applicants in Nigeria and Ghana take their documents to a Visa Facilitation Services Global (VFS) office, which passes them to the embassy visa office. Kenyan applicants hand theirs in at the VFS submission centre in Nairobi, according to the Embassy of Ireland in Kenya.

| Country | Where documents go | What the embassy page says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nigeria | A VFS office in Nigeria, which sends the application to the Embassy of Ireland in Abuja | Fingerprints are taken for applicants aged five and over. Visa fees are non-refundable, and VFS charges its own fee on top. |
| Ghana | A VFS office in Ghana, which sends the application to the Embassy of Ireland in Abuja | The same process as Nigeria. |
| Kenya | The VFS submission centre in Nairobi, for Kenyan and Kenyan-resident applicants, from 15 May 2026 | The embassy asks you to carry copies of your submitted documents when you travel, in case immigration officers inspect them. |

The Abuja page gives the visa fee as the local-currency equivalent of €60 for a single-entry visa, plus the VFS fee. Your Application Summary Form shows the fee and the payment method for your own application. Rely on it over anything you read second-hand, including this guide.

## Which documents does every applicant need?

The Irish Immigration Service lists the same core documents for long-stay study applicants, and says a missing one may lead to refusal. They are on one official page, and the details there are specific. Check each item before you gather anything.

- A signed Application Summary Form and the fee
- Your passport and photographs
- A letter of acceptance and proof of fee payment from your college
- Evidence of your academic ability and of your English
- Private medical insurance
- An application letter
- Details of your finances
- A note on any gap in your education, and on any earlier visa refusal

A college letter has to confirm full-time study with at least 15 hours of daytime tuition a week, for example, and it has to state the fees.

If a required document is missing, the Immigration Service says your application may be refused. Our guide to [why study visas are refused after an offer](/guides/why-study-visas-are-refused-after-an-offer) covers the other problems the Irish authorities name.

## What rules apply to the documents themselves?

Documents must be original, legible and verifiable, in English or Irish or with a full certified translation. Beyond that the two Irish sources differ slightly, so follow the instructions on your own Application Summary Form first. Don't assume the rules for one office apply to another.

- **Irish Immigration Service:** send original documents on paper. It does not accept photocopies except where it says so, and it can't open files on USB sticks, memory cards or file-sharing sites.
- **Abuja embassy:** send originals together with photocopies of every document, including every page of your passport, and keep the originals and photocopies separate. Don't put documents in envelopes or folders. Include a prepaid return envelope from the VFS office.
- **Letters from organisations:** these must be originals on headed paper with a named contact person and a written signature. Electronic signatures and Yahoo or Hotmail addresses are not accepted.

## How much money does Ireland ask students to show?

Visa-required students on academic courses starting after 1 July 2023 must show immediate access to at least €10,000. Ireland gives that as the estimated cost of living for one year. Later years need ready access to the same amount, on top of course fees. Courses under eight months use €833 a month, or €6,665 if that is lower.

The Irish Immigration Service sets out which evidence it accepts on its student finances page. Read that page directly, because the figures and the accepted evidence are updated from time to time.

BloomAbroad doesn't prepare, source or advise on financial documents, and we will never ask you for money to "show funds". Be cautious of anyone who offers to.

## How long does a decision take, and how do I follow it?

The Irish Immigration Service says applicants can expect a decision within about eight weeks of the application reaching the visa office, embassy or consulate. It can take longer when documents are missing or need checking. Your application is processed in the order it arrives.

Two places to look. The Abuja embassy publishes weekly decision reports. The Immigration Service's visa decisions page lists the dates it is currently processing for each visa type at the Dublin visa office and publishes decisions every Tuesday. It says processing times can change during the year, including before a new college year.

If your application is refused, the letter states the reasons and whether you can appeal. Our refusals guide explains the appeal steps and the two-month deadline.
