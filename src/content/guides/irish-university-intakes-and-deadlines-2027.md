## When are the main intakes at Irish universities?

September is the main intake, and most courses start then. Some taught master's courses also start in January, for example at DCU, the University of Limerick and UCD. UCD says there is no January intake for its full-time undergraduate courses. The months a course offers are listed on its own course page.

DCU's January 2027 postgraduate intake covers a short list of master's programmes in business, engineering and computing, and only general entry is available in that intake. TU Dublin lists some programmes with a January start as well, with an age rule that differs from its September one.

If you are open to January, ask each university which of your courses start then. The January lists are short.

## Do international students apply through the CAO?

Usually not. The CAO handles undergraduate applications from residents of the EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland, and its 2027 application facility closes on 1 February at 17:00. Most applicants living elsewhere apply directly to each university. Your fee status decides how you apply, and residence counts for more than nationality.

Trinity bases the decision on where you live. UCD admits undergraduates with EU fee status only through the CAO. TU Dublin sends two groups to the CAO whatever their nationality: applicants with the Irish Leaving Certificate or a QQI FET award, and non-EU nationals who have lived in Ireland for three of the last five years.

Both Trinity and UCD publish a tool for checking your fee status. Use it before you apply, because the method of application and the requirements both depend on it.

## Why should visa-required applicants aim earlier than the final deadline?

Several universities set an earlier cut-off for applicants who need a visa, and the visa process needs time of its own. UL's visa-required deadline for Autumn 2026 was 1 July, about six weeks before its EU deadline. The Irish Immigration Service expects a decision about eight weeks after your application reaches the visa office.

The visa steps also depend on the university. You need a letter of acceptance and your fees paid to the required level before you can apply for the visa. You can apply up to three months before you travel, and the Immigration Service advises you not to buy a ticket before the decision. Our guide to [studying in Ireland from Nigeria](/guides/studying-in-ireland-from-nigeria) sets out the order of those steps.

TU Dublin gives a rough calendar of its own. It suggests preparing for an English test and gathering documents 9 to 12 months before you apply, and submitting your application 9 to 10 months before the course starts.
## What happens if a course fills before the deadline?

It can close early. TU Dublin says some courses close before the published date when they reach capacity, and UL says many programmes close in advance of their date or when places are filled. Trinity admits on a rolling basis after 1 May until 30 June.

That makes the order of your applications matter. Trinity charges €55 for each course application and treats the first as your first preference. UCD charges €70 and lets you apply to several programmes for the same intake once you have paid. Fees add up quickly, so choose the courses you would actually accept.

## What should I have ready, and what does it cost?

Expect to upload your passport, your academic transcripts and certificates and, where required, an English test result. TU Dublin also asks for a grading scale and an up-to-date CV. Rules on late documents differ. TU Dublin lets you apply first and upload missing documents later, while UCD says incomplete applications will not be considered.

Documents that are not in English need an official translation at UCD, and the Irish Immigration Service asks for a full certified translation with your visa application. Our guide to [English tests for Irish universities](/guides/english-tests-for-irish-universities) covers which test to take and how long a result stays valid.
