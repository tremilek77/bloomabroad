## 2. What the research says

- **Trust is the conversion currency** for international students and families. Sites that feel like
  a sales pitch lose them; clear answers to the top questions, easy to find, win
  ([educations.com](https://institutions.educations.com/insights/is-your-website-optimized-for-study-abroad-students),
  [ICEF](https://monitor.icef.com/2024/09/university-websites-how-can-we-make-them-better-for-international-student-recruitment/)).
- **Segment by audience and stage.** One page per audience and a different answer for each market is the
  recommended pattern, which the audience split now follows.
- **Immigration and education advice is "Your Money or Your Life" content.** The common guidance:
  show who wrote or reviewed it and their credentials, cite sources, show review dates, keep a visible
  update history
  ([Yoast](https://yoast.com/what-is-e-e-a-t/), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/?p=380569)).
  Google says E-E-A-T is not itself a ranking factor, so treat this as quality, not a trick.
- **The Irish context is changing, and explaining it is an authority opportunity.** The immigration
  service's policy statement says education providers recruiting non-EU students who need study
  permission must hold the TrustEd Ireland mark, which replaces the Interim List of Eligible Programmes,
  and that the mark does not guarantee every course qualifies
  ([Irish Immigration policy statement](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Policy-Statement-TrustEd-Ireland-and-Immigration-Requirements.pdf)).
  Few agent sites explain this plainly. **[official]**
- **Fraud is a live concern.** An Garda Síochána's 25 Aug 2026 student warning covers rental scams
  (230+ reports, €400,000+ lost to 31 July 2026) and money-mule approaches
  ([garda.ie](https://www.garda.ie/en/about-us/our-departments/office-of-corporate-communications/press-releases/2026/august/25-08-2026-an-garda-siochana-issues-student-fraud-warning-ahead-of-cao-offers-25th-august-2026.html)).
  It fits the /verify "stay safe" positioning. **[official]**
- **Refusal figures are searched for and mostly badly sourced.** Third-party sites quote 1 to 4% with no
  source. The Irish Examiner (17 Oct 2026) reports Department of Justice figures of 506 refusals from
  1,393 Nigerian long-stay study applications in 2026 so far (36%), and 445 of 1,106 in 2025 (40%)
  ([Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/news/arid-41925241.html)). Use only after checking
  the Department's own figures, and never as a claim about BloomAbroad's results. **[official]**
- The search did not find reliable official intake dates, English-test minimums or Ghana and Kenya
  refusal figures. Those must come from each institution or the Department. Nothing here is a
  fact to publish until it is checked.

### C. Needs facts or permission from Remi

- **Real numbers.** "10+ years" is the only figure. Credible, checkable ones (students supported,
  countries, institutions) would lift every page. Do not publish any that cannot be evidenced.
- **Testimonials** ("In their words") with written consent, per the brief's rules.
- **Anonymised case patterns** for the refusals guide.
- **Photos**, the **60-second walkthrough video**, and **Dr Kolawole's agreement** for `/about`.
- **Founder profile links** (LinkedIn or similar) so the founder and adviser can be verified off-site.
- **Company facts**: CRO number, registered office, EU data region. Visible company details are a
  trust signal.

## 4. What I'd not do

- Quote the third-party "1 to 4% refusal rate" anywhere.
- Add outcome claims ("improves conversion", "cuts refusals") without pilot or study evidence.
- Add Kenya or Ghana pages before Stripe, prices and local guides are real.
- Name partner organisations that the brief says not to name.
- Pad pages for word count. The goal is depth behind the scannable layer, not longer pages.

## Status, 23 Nov 2026

**Decision:** no sample report. It could drift from what the real system produces. The site describes what
the report covers (outcome, what is strong, what is missing, next steps, a summary for parents and
sponsors) and shows no example applicant. The earlier fictional "Amara" card on /students is gone, and
`READY.sample` stays false.

**Built:** `/how-we-assess`, `/enrolment-readiness` (with glossary), homepage depth, `/students` report
and fit sections, comparison table on `/pricing`, and a "Last updated · reading time · Spotted a mistake?"
bar on every marketing page. Dates live in `src/content/updated.ts`: change a page's date whenever you
change its wording or facts.

**Voice rule:** every new line is checked against the copywriting skill's AI-tells list, and
`scripts/check-voice.mjs` runs at the end of every build. It fails on contrast reveals, negation lists,
stock openers, banned vocabulary, em dashes and exclamation points, and warns on long sentences and
staccato runs. It cannot predict an AI detector's score, because those tools are unreliable and disagree.
The strongest human signal is real material only the client has: Remi's own words, numbers she can back
up, quotes with consent.

**Still needs Remi:** real numbers, testimonials, photos, Dr Kolawole's agreement for `/about`, and a
read of the glossary and `/how-we-assess` before they go live.
